# Modeled level-65 equipment

The modeled equipment benchmark is a **what-if scenario**, not a list of obtainable WoW Forever loot. Synthetic item IDs begin at `910000001`, and item names start with **“Modeled:”**. Source IDs and item-allocation assumptions are preserved in `assets/db_inputs/forever_synthetic_gear.json`. The verified, available item records remain separate in `assets/db_inputs/forever_gear_catalog.json`.

The slot-coordinate search covered 28 builds and all 184 available race/build profiles. All 184 candidates cleared an independent, matched 5,000-iteration confirmation; two Fury (Sunder) profiles required one further pass and converged without another gear change. Selected profiles contain 1,604 modeled item slots (8.7 per profile on average) alongside real items. A real item below level 65 can still win on modeled DPS; the search does not force its replacement merely to show a higher item level. Exact trial and confirmation evidence is in the [search archive](../artifacts/modelled_gear_search/current/summary.json). These comparisons do not establish the accuracy of projected prices, future availability, or a global equipment optimum.

The [proposed gear spreadsheet](../artifacts/modelled_gear/proposed_gear.xlsx) has editable allocations, source links, armor/weapon proofs and coverage gaps. Regenerate it with `python3 tools/database/export_forever_synthetic_workbook.py` after installing the optional Python `XlsxWriter` package; that package is not needed to build the simulator.

The level-65 client’s stat-quantity, base armor and weapon-damage tables are pinned under `assets/db_inputs/forever_model_tables/`. Run `python3 tools/database/generate_forever_synthetic_gear.py`, then `python3 tools/database/compile_forever_equipment.py`, then `go run ./tools/database/gen_db -outDir=assets -gen=db` to rebuild the separate model and compiled database. The source formula exactly reconstructs 1,217 catalog armor records and 178 catalog weapon damage ranges, but does **not** prove future server item allocations or the availability of the synthetic items.

Proposed armor limits the modeled Stamina cost to 20% of its estimated item budget and pays for any removed Stamina with an absent primary/Hit/Crit allocation. Source allocations of attack power, spell power, school power and MP5 are not exchanged into other special stats. Cross-slot templates are scaled downward when their *assumed* special-stat price rises. These exchange prices are approximations, not verified Forever itemization rules. This is distinct from the benchmark’s paid shared-hit convention; hit normalization must be recalculated on every candidate.

There is no confirmed crafted/PvP level-65 passive-stat trinket, neck or ring. Trinkets at 80% of the projected allocation are eligible for the principal search; the full-allocation trinkets are excluded from rankings and retained only as sensitivity references. Synthetic rings, necks, off-hand daggers and caster one-hand weapons remain hypothetical. Stats, armor types, hand restrictions and actual source links can be inspected in the local workbook and generated catalog. A modeled item inherits **neither** its reference item's acquisition, profession requirement, ordinary set bonus nor on-use/proc effect. Confirm real class/equipment legality separately.

Item icons come from existing in-game art for the corresponding slot and armor material. An icon reference can be an otherwise unavailable real item; that art does not establish the availability or stats of the modeled item.

The gear selector compares projected body armor in each class's usual armor material. It omits an otherwise identical projected lower-armor copy for that class; this is a candidate-pool convention, **not** a rule forbidding a character from wearing legal lower-armor equipment. Real source items remain eligible under their ordinary proficiency restrictions.

No baseline class talents, APLs, race availability, external buffs, consumables, encounter settings or forced role-specific Tier 1 bonuses change in this gear scenario. The prior real-item benchmark remains separate. DPS comparisons across the two scenarios measure the modeled loadouts and these explicit assumptions, not actual obtainable gear or class balance at launch.
